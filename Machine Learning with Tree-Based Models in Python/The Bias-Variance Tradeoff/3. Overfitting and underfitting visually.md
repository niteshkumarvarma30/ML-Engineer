## Overfitting and underfitting visually

### Concept: Identifying High Bias and High Variance
You can often diagnose whether a model is suffering from High Bias or High Variance simply by plotting its predictions over the actual data.

* **High Variance (Overfitting):** The prediction line looks highly erratic, jagged, and chaotic. It zig-zags wildly to perfectly hit every single training data point. It has memorized the noise.
* **High Bias (Underfitting):** The prediction line looks rigid, flat, or stubbornly simple. It completely ignores obvious curves or trends in the data, predicting constant or near-constant values.

### Question
For this purpose, we have trained two different models $A$ and $B$ on the auto dataset to predict the `mpg` consumption of a car using only the car's displacement (`displ`) as a feature.

The following figure shows you scatterplots of `mpg` versus `displ` along with lines corresponding to the training set predictions of models $A$ and $B$ in red.

*(Model A shows a highly erratic, jagged red line that spikes up and down to hit individual blue dots perfectly).*
*(Model B shows a very simple step-function red line that stays flat for a long time, completely missing the overall downward curve of the dots).*

Which of the following statements is true?

**Possible Answers:**
1. $A$ suffers from high bias and overfits the training set.
2. $A$ suffers from high variance and underfits the training set.
3. $B$ suffers from high bias and underfits the training set.
4. $B$ suffers from high variance and underfits the training set.

### Solution

**Correct Answer: 3**

*$B$ suffers from high bias and underfits the training set.*

**Explanation:**
Model $B$ is far too simple. It uses a single split to divide the data into two flat horizontal lines, completely ignoring the continuous downward trend in the data points. Because it is too simple to capture the true pattern, it suffers from High Bias (Underfitting). 

*(Note: Model $A$ is the exact opposite—it is highly chaotic, tracing every single outlier, meaning it suffers from High Variance / Overfitting).*
