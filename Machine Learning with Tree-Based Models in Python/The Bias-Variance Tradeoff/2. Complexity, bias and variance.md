## Complexity, bias and variance

### Concept: The Bias-Variance Seesaw
There is a direct relationship between the complexity of your model ($\hat{f}$) and the Bias-Variance tradeoff. They sit on a seesaw controlled by complexity.

* **Increasing Complexity** (e.g., a deeper decision tree): The model becomes highly flexible. It can perfectly trace the patterns in the training data, so **Bias decreases**. However, because it is so flexible, it also memorizes the random noise, making it highly sensitive to new data, so **Variance increases**.
* **Decreasing Complexity** (e.g., a very shallow tree): The model becomes rigid. It ignores the noise, meaning its predictions are stable (**Variance decreases**), but it's too simple to capture the real underlying pattern, so it makes consistently inaccurate predictions (**Bias increases**).

### Question
In the video, you saw how the complexity of a model labeled $\hat{f}$ influences the bias and variance terms of its generalization error. Which of the following correctly describes the relationship between $\hat{f}$'s complexity and $\hat{f}$'s bias and variance terms?

**Possible Answers:**
1. As the complexity of $\hat{f}$ decreases, the bias term decreases while the variance term increases.
2. As the complexity of $\hat{f}$ decreases, both the bias and the variance terms increase.
3. As the complexity of $\hat{f}$ increases, the bias term increases while the variance term decreases.
4. As the complexity of $\hat{f}$ increases, the bias term decreases while the variance term increases.

### Solution

**Correct Answer: 4**

*As the complexity of $\hat{f}$ increases, the bias term decreases while the variance term increases.*

**Explanation:**
This perfectly describes the tradeoff! A highly complex model is flexible enough to capture the true underlying signal (low bias), but it is also flexible enough to accidentally memorize the random noise in the training set (high variance).
