# Concept

## Diagnosing Bias and Variance

We already know the theory behind the Bias-Variance Tradeoff.

* **High Bias (Underfitting):** The model is too simple and misses the pattern.
* **High Variance (Overfitting):** The model is too complex and memorizes the noise.

But how do you actually know which problem your model has in the real world? We can't see the "True" function. Here is how we diagnose the patient!

---

### 1. The "Locked Vault" Problem

To know if our model is good, we want to know its **Generalization Error** (how many mistakes it will make in the real world on unseen data).

* **Why not use the Training Data?** If we test the model on the data it just studied, it will cheat. It will give us a highly optimistic (fake) score.
* **Why not use the Test Data?** The Test Set is the final exam. We must lock it in a vault and never touch it until we are 100% finished tuning our model. If we peek at it early, we ruin our experiment.

So, if we can't test on the Training Data, and we aren't allowed to touch the Test Data... how do we grade the model? We use **Cross-Validation**.

---

### 2. The Solution: K-Fold Cross-Validation (CV)

K-Fold CV is a clever trick to grade the model using *only* the training data.

Imagine a teacher gives a student 10 chapters of a textbook to study (The Training Data).

1. The teacher chops the book into 10 chunks (or folds, $K=10$).
2. The teacher takes away Chapter 1, and tells the student to study Chapters 2 through 10.
3. The teacher then gives the student a quiz on Chapter 1. (We record the error).
4. The teacher resets the student's brain, takes away Chapter 2, and tells them to study 1, 3, 4...10. Then gives a quiz on Chapter 2.
5. This repeats 10 times.

At the end, we take the Average (Mean) of those 10 quiz scores. This is our **CV-Error**. It is a highly accurate estimate of how the model will perform in the real world!

---

### 3. Diagnosing the Patient

Now that we have two scores (the **Training Error** and the **CV-Error**), we can easily diagnose our model!

#### Diagnosis A: High Variance (Overfitting)

* **The Symptoms:** The Training Error is very low (it got an A+ on the homework), but the CV-Error is very high (it failed the quizzes).
* **The Cure:** Decrease the model's complexity!
    * If it's a Decision Tree, reduce the `max_depth` or increase the `min_samples_leaf` to stop it from memorizing the data.
    * *Alternative cure:* Go out and gather more training data!

#### Diagnosis B: High Bias (Underfitting)

* **The Symptoms:** The CV-Error is roughly equal to the Training Error, but *both* of them are terrible (much higher than your desired error).
* **The Cure:** Increase the model's complexity!
    * If it's a Decision Tree, increase the `max_depth` to give it more flexibility.
    * *Alternative cure:* Gather more features (new columns/clues) to help the model learn better patterns.

---

### 4. Cheat Sheet: Scikit-Learn Code

Here is how you perform a 10-Fold Cross-Validation in Python.

> [!NOTE]
> Scikit-learn has a quirky rule where "higher scores are always better". To make errors fit this rule, it uses Negative Mean Squared Error (`neg_mean_squared_error`). We just multiply the result by `-1` to flip it back into a normal, positive error score!

```python
import numpy as np
from sklearn.tree import DecisionTreeRegressor
from sklearn.model_selection import train_test_split, cross_val_score
from sklearn.metrics import mean_squared_error as MSE

# 1. Split the data (Lock 30% in the vault!)

# 2. Instantiate the model
dt = DecisionTreeRegressor(max_depth=4, min_samples_leaf=0.14, random_state=1)

# 3. Perform 10-Fold Cross-Validation
# cv=10 sets the 10 folds. 
# n_jobs=-1 tells the computer to use ALL of its CPU cores to do the math much faster!
MSE_CV_scores = cross_val_score(dt, X_train, y_train, cv=10, 
                                scoring='neg_mean_squared_error',
                                n_jobs=-1)

# 4. Flip the negative signs back to positive, and calculate the Average CV-Error
cv_error = np.mean(MSE_CV_scores * -1)

print(f"Average CV Error: {cv_error:.2f}")

# 5. Fit to the training data and check the Training Error to make your diagnosis!
dt.fit(X_train, y_train)
y_pred_train = dt.predict(X_train)
train_error = MSE(y_train, y_pred_train)

print(f"Training Error: {train_error:.2f}")

# ==========================================
# DIAGNOSIS: 
# ==========================================
# If Train Error < CV Error 
#   --> High Variance (Overfitting)
#
# If Train Error is roughly equal to CV Error, but both are too high 
#   --> High Bias (Underfitting)
```
