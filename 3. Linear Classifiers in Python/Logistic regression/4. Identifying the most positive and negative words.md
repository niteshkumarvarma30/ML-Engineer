## Identifying the most positive and negative words

### Concept: Interpreting Coefficients
In this exercise we'll try to interpret the coefficients of a logistic regression fit on the movie review sentiment dataset. The model object is already instantiated and fit for you in the variable `lr`.

When a model assigns a large positive weight (e.g. +5.0) to a word, it means that word heavily influences the model to predict a "Positive Review". When it assigns a large negative weight (e.g. -5.0), the word heavily pushes the model toward predicting a "Negative Review".

The words corresponding to the different features are loaded into the variable `vocab`. For example, since `vocab[100]` is `"think"`, that means feature 100 corresponds to the number of times the word `"think"` appeared in that movie review.

---

### Instructions

1. Find the words corresponding to the 5 largest coefficients (the most positive words).
2. Find the words corresponding to the 5 smallest coefficients (the most negative words).

### Solution

```python
import numpy as np

# Get the indices of the sorted coefficients
# np.argsort() sorts from smallest (most negative) to largest (most positive)
inds_ascending = np.argsort(lr.coef_.flatten()) 
inds_descending = inds_ascending[::-1]

# Print the most positive words (largest coefficients)
print("Most positive words: ", end="")
for i in range(5):
    # Use the descending indices array
    print(vocab[inds_descending[i]], end=", ")
print("\n")

# Print most negative words (smallest coefficients)
print("Most negative words: ", end="")
for i in range(5):
    # Use the ascending indices array
    print(vocab[inds_ascending[i]], end=", ")
print("\n")
```

**Takeaway:** This is why linear models like Logistic Regression are so highly valued in business and medicine! Unlike "black box" deep learning neural networks, you can simply look at the coefficients (`lr.coef_`) to see exactly *why* the model made its decision.
