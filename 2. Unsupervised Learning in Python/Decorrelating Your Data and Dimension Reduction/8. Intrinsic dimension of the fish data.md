## Intrinsic dimension of the fish data

### Concept: Reading the Scree Plot

In the previous exercise, you used `plt.bar()` to plot the variance of the PCA features of the fish measurements. This created a **Scree Plot**.

Recall from our guide that the *intrinsic dimension* is the number of PCA features with "significant" variance. You find it by looking for the "elbow" in the Scree Plot—the point where the tall bars suddenly drop off into a long, flat tail of tiny bars. The tall bars represent the true shape of the data (signal), while the tiny bars represent insignificant information (noise).

### Instructions

In the previous exercise, you plotted the variance of the PCA features of the fish measurements. Looking again at your plot, what do you think would be a reasonable choice for the "intrinsic dimension" of the fish measurements?

**Possible answers:**
1. 1
2. 2
3. 5

*(Hint: Look at the generated bar chart. How many bars are significantly taller than the rest before the plot flatlines near zero?)*

---

### Solution

**Correct Answer: 2**

**Explanation:**
If you look at the bar chart generated in the previous exercise, you will see a massive drop-off after the second bar. 
* PCA feature 0 has a very high variance (around 4.0).
* PCA feature 1 has a significant variance (around 1.8).
* PCA feature 2 instantly drops to nearly 0 (around 0.2).
* PCA features 3, 4, and 5 are practically invisible.

Because only the first **2** PCA features contain significant variance, the intrinsic dimension of the fish dataset is 2. This means you could safely compress this 6-dimensional dataset down to just 2 dimensions without losing the core information!
