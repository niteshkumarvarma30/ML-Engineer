# Coarse to Fine Tuning

Up until now, we have used Grid Search and Random Search. In the machine learning world, these are considered **Uninformed Searches**.

Why are they "Uninformed"? Because they do not learn as they go. If a Grid Search tests a `max_depth` of 2 and gets a terrible score, it doesn't learn from that mistake. It will stubbornly go on to test every other combination that includes a `max_depth` of 2 anyway.

To save time and computational power, we need an **Informed Search**: a method that actually learns from its previous tests!

## 1. What is Coarse to Fine Tuning?

*(Look at your Informed vs Uninformed screenshot).*
Instead of doing one massive, blind search all at once, an informed search builds models sequentially in a loop: **Learn ➔ Measure ➔ One Model ➔ Learn...**

"Coarse to Fine" is the most basic informed search strategy.

**The Analogy:** Imagine you are looking for a lost ring on a massive beach.

*   **Coarse:** First, you use a metal detector and run quickly across the entire beach (a wide, rough search).
*   **Fine:** When the detector beeps in one specific corner, you get on your hands and knees and sift through the sand with a tiny scoop (a deep, highly focused search).

## 2. The 4-Step Process

*(Look at your Coarse to Fine Tuning screenshot).* Here is how we execute this strategy in Python:

1.  **Random Search (The Wide Net):** Start with a massive hyperparameter grid. Instead of testing all 134,400 possible combinations, run a quick Random Search on just 500 models to survey the landscape.
2.  **Find Promising Areas:** Look at the results of those 500 models. Where did the highest scores come from?
3.  **Grid Search (The Microscope):** Create a brand new, much smaller hyperparameter grid focused strictly around the winning areas from Step 2. Run an exhaustive Grid Search on this tiny area!
4.  **Continue:** Repeat this process until you find the absolute best score.

*(Note: Coarse to Fine is informed on "batches" of models, rather than learning from every single individual model one-by-one).*

## 3. How to Find the Promising Areas (Visualizing)

Step 2 is the most important part of this process. How do we actually know where the "good" areas are?

If you just look at a table of the top 5 models *(see your Visualizing Coarse to Fine - Top results screenshot)*, it is really hard for a human to spot the overarching trend.

**The Solution: Scatter Plots!**
*(Look at the scatter plot screenshots).* We plot every single one of our 500 random guesses on a graph: Hyperparameter Value (X-axis) vs. Accuracy Score (Y-axis).

Suddenly, the trends become blindingly obvious!

*   **Analyzing `max_depth`:** Looking at the graph, all the dots below a depth of 8 have terrible accuracy. All the dots above a depth of 30 drop off in accuracy. The "sweet spot" is clearly between 8 and 30!
*   **Analyzing `min_samples_leaf`:** Looking at its graph, the highest accuracy blue dots are clustered on the left side. The accuracy gets consistently worse as the number goes above 8.
*   **Analyzing `learn_rate`:** (From the transcript), models performed terribly when the learning rate went above 1.3.

## 4. The Next Iteration

Now that we have visually analyzed our "Coarse" random search, we can build our "Fine" grid search!

Instead of testing 134,400 combinations like we started with, we can narrow our menu down to:

*   **`max_depth`:** between 8 and 30
*   **`min_samples_leaf`:** below 8
*   **`learn_rate`:** below 1.3

By throwing away the bad areas of the map, our next search will run infinitely faster and hone in on the absolute perfect model!
