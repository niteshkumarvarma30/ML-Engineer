## Building a Recommender System with NMF

Have you ever read a news article, and at the bottom of the page, the website says, *"You might also like these articles..."*?

That is a **Recommender System**. Using Non-Negative Matrix Factorization (NMF), we can teach a computer to automatically read thousands of articles, figure out what they are about, and recommend similar ones.

Here is exactly how the pipeline works, step-by-step.

---

### Step 1: Discovering the "Smoothie Ingredients" (NMF)

Computers can't read English, so we first turn all the articles into a massive spreadsheet of word counts (a TF-IDF matrix).

Then, we unleash NMF on that spreadsheet.
Imagine every article is a blended smoothie, and NMF is a master chef trying to guess the base ingredients (the **Hidden Topics**).

* **Topic 1:** AI / Tech
* **Topic 2:** Sports
* **Topic 3:** Politics

Once NMF finds the topics, it rewrites every single article as a recipe (these are the **NMF Features**).

* **Article A:** 80% Tech, 20% Politics, 0% Sports.
* **Article B:** 0% Tech, 95% Sports, 5% Politics.

---

### Step 2: The "Serving Size" Problem (Normalization)

We have a slight math problem.

* **Article C** is a massive, 10,000-word mega-essay about Artificial Intelligence.
* **Article D** is a tiny, 100-word news brief about Artificial Intelligence.

Because Article C has way more words, its math vector is going to be massive. If the computer compares them raw, it will think they are completely different just because of their size.

#### The Solution: Normalization
Normalization forces every article to be the exact same "serving size" (a vector length of 1). 
It doesn't change the flavor of the smoothie (the ratio of topics), it just shrinks the 10-gallon bucket and the 1-cup glass down to the exact same size. Now, the computer can easily see that both articles are exactly 100% Artificial Intelligence!

---

### Step 3: The Matchmaker (Cosine Similarity)

Now that all our articles are the exact same size, how do we measure how "similar" they are? We use **Cosine Similarity**.

Imagine drawing each article as an arrow on a graph, based on its recipe:

* If Article X and Article Y point in the exact same direction, the angle between them is $0^\circ$. Their Cosine Similarity is **1.0 (A perfect match!)**.
* If they point in completely opposite directions, they have nothing in common. Their Cosine Similarity is **0.0**.

> **Data Science Math Trick:** Because we already Normalized the vectors in Step 2, we can calculate the Cosine Similarity instantly by just multiplying the vectors together using a Dot Product (`.dot()`).

---

### Step 4: The Final Python Pipeline

Here is how you write this entire pipeline in Python using scikit-learn and pandas:

```python
from sklearn.decomposition import NMF
from sklearn.preprocessing import normalize
```
