# Sequence Generation in NLP

In our previous lessons, we looked at AI that acts like a detective—reading text to classify it (Text Classification) or finding an exact answer hiding inside a paragraph (Extractive QA).

But what if we want the AI to act like a writer? This is called **Sequence Generation**.

![Sequence Generation](images/Screenshot%202026-07-20%20153253.png)

Sequence generation is the process of producing completely new text based on a given input. It doesn't just copy and paste; it digests the information and creates something brand new.

Here are the three most popular ways we use Sequence Generation in the real world.

## 1. Text Summarization (The "TL;DR" Maker)

![Text Summarization](images/Screenshot%202026-07-20%20153308.png)

Text Summarization takes a massive, lengthy document and condenses it into a short, easy-to-read paragraph.

Imagine you are a busy executive, and someone hands you a 50-page research report. A summarization model reads the entire report, throws away the redundant fluff, and hands you a 3-sentence summary of the absolute most important facts. It is perfect for digesting news articles, legal documents, and long emails!

### Summarization in Python

![Summarization in Python](images/Screenshot%202026-07-20%20153320.png)

To do this in Python, we use a Hugging Face pipeline with the task set to `"summarization"`.

```python
from transformers import pipeline

# 1. Define the Summarization Pipeline
summarizer = pipeline(
    task="summarization", 
    model="cnicu/t5-small-booksum"
)

# 2. Provide a Long Paragraph
text = "The Amazon rainforest, often referred to as the 'lungs of the Earth,' is one of the most biologically diverse regions in the world. Spanning over nine countries in South America, the majority of the forest lies in Brazil. It is home to an estimated 390 billion individual trees, divided into 16,000 different species. The rainforest plays a critical role in regulating the global climate by absorbing vast amounts of carbon dioxide and producing oxygen."

# 3. Ask the Model to Summarize!
result = summarizer(text)
print(result)

# Output: [{'summary_text': 'the Amazon rainforest is one of the most biologically diverse regions in the world. The majority of the forest lies in Brazil. The rainforest plays a critical role in regulating the global climate by absorbing vast amounts of carbon dioxide and producing oxygen.'}]
```

**Understanding the Output:** The model successfully cut out the extra trivia (like the exact number of trees and species) and returned just the core facts in a clean `summary_text` string!

## 2. Text Translation (The "Babel Fish")

![Text Translation](images/Screenshot%202026-07-20%20153336.png)

Text Translation is exactly what it sounds like: converting text from one language into another.

Because grammar and sentence structure change drastically between languages (for example, adjectives come after nouns in Spanish, but before nouns in English), the AI must generate a completely new sequence of words rather than just swapping them one-by-one.

### Translation in Python

![Translation in Python](images/Screenshot%202026-07-20%20153347.png)

To translate text, we change our pipeline task to `"translation"`.
*Note: You must pick a model that was specifically trained on the two languages you want to use! In this case, we use `opus-mt-en-fr` to go from English (en) to French (fr).*

```python
from transformers import pipeline

# 1. Define the Translation Pipeline (English to French)
translator = pipeline(
    task="translation", 
    model="Helsinki-NLP/opus-mt-en-fr"
)

# 2. Provide the English Sentence
sentence = "The rainforest helps regulate the Earth's climate."

# 3. Translate!
result = translator(sentence)
print(result)

# Output: [{'translation_text': "La forêt tropicale aide à réguler le climat de la Terre."}]
```

## 3. Language Modeling (The "Autocomplete" Brain)

![Language Modeling](images/Screenshot%202026-07-20%20153356.png)

Have you ever used Gmail's "Smart Compose," where it guesses the rest of your sentence as you type? Or used ChatGPT to write a story? This is powered by Language Modeling.

The goal of Language Modeling is simple: Look at a prompt, and predict the next logical word. By predicting the next word over and over again, it generates a fluent, creative continuation of your text.

### Language Modeling in Python

![Language Modeling in Python](images/Screenshot%202026-07-20%20153405.png)

To do this, we use the `"text-generation"` pipeline.

Because this model is "creative", we can give it two extra rules (hyperparameters) to control its creativity:
*   `max_length=30`: Stop writing after 30 tokens so it doesn't ramble forever.
*   `num_return_sequences=3`: Write 3 completely different, unique stories using the exact same prompt!

```python
from transformers import pipeline

# 1. Define the Text Generation Pipeline
generator = pipeline(
    task="text-generation", 
    model="distilgpt2"
)

# 2. Provide the Prompt (The starting thought)
prompt = "Once upon a time,"

# 3. Generate the Stories!
result = generator(prompt, max_length=30, num_return_sequences=3)
print(result)

# Output 1: "Once upon a time, my life wasn't so good, I kept my things tidy..."
# Output 2: "Once upon a time, we began a process of finding the right answers..."
# Output 3: "Once upon a time, a man came along and took in the city..."
```

**Understanding the Output:** From a simple 4-word prompt, the AI successfully predicted and generated three completely unique, grammatically correct continuations!
