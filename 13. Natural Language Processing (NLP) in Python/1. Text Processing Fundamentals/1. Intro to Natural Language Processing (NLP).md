# Intro to Natural Language Processing (NLP)

![Course plan](../../images/Screenshot%202026-07-19%20195126.png)

Computers are essentially just giant calculators. They are amazing at doing math on numbers, but they cannot naturally read a book, understand a tweet, or figure out if an email is spam.

Natural Language Processing (NLP) is the process of acting as a translator. It takes human language (English, Spanish, etc.) and translates it into a mathematical format that a computer can actually understand and analyze.

## 1. The NLP Workflow

![NLP workflow](../../images/Screenshot%202026-07-19%20194851.png)

To turn text into math, we follow a strict 4-step assembly line:

*   **Raw Text:** You grab the text you want to analyze. This could be a 140-character tweet or a 1,000-page novel.
*   **Preprocessing:** You clean the text. You throw away useless garbage (like weird symbols or formatting) to make the text as clean as possible.
*   **Feature Extraction:** This is the translation step! You take the clean text and convert it entirely into numbers (vectors/arrays).
*   **Model:** You feed those numbers into a machine learning algorithm. The computer can now do math on those numbers to classify the text (e.g., Spam vs. Not Spam) or even generate new text!

## 2. Step 1 of Preprocessing: Tokenization

![Tokenization](../../images/Screenshot%202026-07-19%20195229.png)

You can't just hand a massive 1,000-page book to a computer all at once. You have to chop it up into smaller, digestible pieces first.

As the instructor said: "Think of it like chopping vegetables. Instead of cooking a whole carrot, we slice it into smaller pieces to make it easier to cook."

These chopped-up pieces are called **Tokens**. There are two main ways to tokenize text using Python's NLTK (Natural Language Toolkit) library.

### A. Sentence Tokenization

![Sentence tokenization](../../images/Screenshot%202026-07-19%20195331.png)

If you have a massive paragraph, you can chop it up into a list of individual sentences. This is highly useful for translation apps (like Google Translate), because translating one sentence at a time is much more accurate than trying to translate a whole page at once.

**The Python Code:**
```python
import nltk
# Download the grammar rules so NLTK knows what a period/question mark is!
nltk.download('punkt_tab') 

text = "NLP is fun. Let's dive into it!"
sentences = nltk.sent_tokenize(text)
print(sentences)

# Output: ["NLP is fun.", "Let's dive into it!"]
```

### B. Word Tokenization

![Word tokenization](../../images/Screenshot%202026-07-19%20195349.png)

If you want to know exactly what a sentence is about, you need to chop it down even further into individual words and punctuation marks. This allows you to count exactly how many times the word "Win" or "Free" appears, which is how Spam Filters work!

**The Python Code:**
```python
text = "Claim your free prize now!"
words = nltk.word_tokenize(text)
print(words)

# Output: ['Claim', 'your', 'free', 'prize', 'now', '!']
```

*(Notice how it completely separated the exclamation point `!` from the word `now`? To the computer, punctuation marks are treated as their own unique tokens!)*
