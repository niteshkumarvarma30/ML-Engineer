## Which decision boundary is linear?

### Concept: Identifying Linear Boundaries visually

A **Linear Classifier** will always separate data by drawing a perfectly straight line (in 2D) or a flat hyperplane (in 3D+). It cannot curve, bend, or zigzag.

In the provided images, we see four different types of decision boundaries separating the blue and red points.

---

### Instructions

**Question:** Which of the following is a linear decision boundary?

**Possible answers:**
* [ ] (1)
* [ ] (2)
* [ ] (3)
* [ ] (4)

---

### Solution

**Correct Answer:** `(1)`

**Why this is correct:**
* **Plot (1)** shows a perfectly straight, diagonal line separating the red and blue regions. This is the hallmark of a linear classifier like Logistic Regression or LinearSVC.
* **Plot (2)** shows a smooth, curved boundary. This is non-linear (likely a standard `SVC` with an RBF kernel).
* **Plot (3)** shows a highly irregular, jagged boundary that creates "islands" around specific data points. This is highly non-linear and indicative of overfitting (likely `KNeighborsClassifier` with a very low `k`).
* **Plot (4)** shows boundaries made of perfectly horizontal and vertical straight lines connecting at right angles. While the individual segments are straight, the overall boundary is not a single straight line. This is the classic step-pattern of a Decision Tree.
