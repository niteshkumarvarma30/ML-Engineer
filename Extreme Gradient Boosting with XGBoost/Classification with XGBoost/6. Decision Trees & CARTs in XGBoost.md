# Concept

## Decision Trees & CARTs in XGBoost

Before we can understand how XGBoost achieves its blazing-fast speed and accuracy, we have to understand the fundamental building blocks it uses.

XGBoost is an **Ensemble Learning** method. It doesn't just build one model; it builds a massive team of models. The individual models that make up this team are called **Base Learners**.

In XGBoost, the base learners are almost always Decision Trees.

---

### 1. The Decision Tree Refresher (The "20 Questions" Game)

As a quick refresher, a Decision Tree doesn't use massive math equations. It plays a game of "20 Questions."

* **The Nodes:** It asks a binary (Yes/No) question. *(e.g., "Does the car have more than 50,000 miles?")*
* **The Splits:** Depending on the answer, the data flows left or right down the branches. The tree chooses its questions specifically to increase the "purity" of the buckets.
* **The Leaves:** The very bottom of the tree. This is where the final decision is made.
* **Stopping Criteria:** The tree keeps asking questions iteratively until it hits a rule you set in advance (like a `max_depth` of 3).

---

### 2. The Flaw: High Variance (Overfitting)

If a Decision Tree is so smart, why do we need XGBoost? Why not just use one massive Decision Tree?

Because single decision trees are **Low-Bias, High-Variance** models.

* **Low-Bias:** They are incredibly flexible and can learn almost any complex pattern.
* **High-Variance (The Flaw):** Because they are so flexible, if you don't constrain them, they will completely memorize the training data (including the random noise). They will get a 100% on the practice test, but fail miserably in the real world.

To fix this, XGBoost uses a *team* of trees to smooth out these errors.

---

### 3. The Big Twist: What is a CART?

Here is the most important new concept from this transcript:

XGBoost doesn't use standard Decision Trees. It specifically uses **CARTs (Classification And Regression Trees)**.

What is the difference? 
Imagine you are building a model to predict if a patient is Sick or Healthy (Classification).

* **Standard Classification Tree:** When a patient lands in a leaf node, the tree takes a majority vote and outputs a hard category. *(e.g., "This leaf says: SICK")*.
* **A CART:** When a patient lands in a leaf node, the tree **always outputs a real-valued number (a score)**, even if you are doing classification! *(e.g., "This leaf says: +2.5" or "This leaf says: -1.2")*.

#### Why does a CART output numbers for categories?

If you are building an ensemble of 100 trees, you can't easily do math with the words "Sick" and "Healthy".

By forcing every tree to output a raw number (a score), XGBoost can easily **add up the scores from all 100 trees** into one massive final number. 

Once it has that final, aggregated number, it applies a **Threshold** to convert it back into a category. *(e.g., "If the total score of all 100 trees is greater than 0, the patient is SICK")*.

This mathematical trick is the entire foundation of how Gradient Boosting works!
